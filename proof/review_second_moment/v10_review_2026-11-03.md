## 5. New finding: Lemma 7(b) is sharp, so $5/6$ is the method's barrier

For prime $n=p\nmid2t$: $a(b)=\big(\tfrac{t^2-4b^2}{p}\big)$, so $\sum_b a(b)^2=p-3$ and Plancherel gives $\sum_\chi|\hat a(\chi)|^2=(p-3)/(p-1)$. For even non-principal $\chi=\psi^2$ the coefficient is a Jacobi sum:
$$\hat a(\chi)=\overline{\psi}(t^2/4)\,\frac{J+\chi_2(-1)\overline J}{p-1},\qquad J=J(\chi_2,\overline\psi),\ |J|=\sqrt p,$$
and $\hat a(\chi)=0$ for odd $\chi$ (verified numerically to $10^{-14}$ per character, `jacobi_probe.log`). Hence $|\hat a(\chi)|\le2\sqrt p/(p-1)$ and
$$\sum_\chi|\hat a(\chi)|\ \ge\ \frac{\sum_\chi|\hat a(\chi)|^2}{\max_\chi|\hat a(\chi)|}=\frac{p-3}{2\sqrt p},$$
so the exponent $1/2$ in Lemma 7(b) is attained at every prime modulus (numerically $\max_t\sum_\chi|\hat a(\chi)|\approx0.63\sqrt p\approx(2/\pi)\sqrt p$, the equidistributed-Jacobi-angle value). Consequently $\sum_{n\le M}n^{-1}\sum_\chi|\hat a_n(\chi)|\gg\sqrt M/\log M$ from primes alone, and the error term of Lemma 8 cannot be improved by sharpening Lemma 7. Going below $\theta=5/6$ would need cancellation between different $n$ or different characters in $\sum_n n^{-1}\sum_\chi\hat a_n(\chi)\sum_p(\log p)g(p)\chi(p)$, not a better second-moment bound. Suggest one sentence to this effect in Remark (3) and/or §Scope (currently "a sharper treatment of either would widen it" — true for item 3, but for item 4 the sharpening would have to be of this joint kind).
